The Fundamental Dilemma
Offline Stale State

When two phones are completely disconnected from the banking network, neither phone knows if the sender has spent funds elsewhere. Both see Alice's initial balance of ₹1,000.

Competing Demands
₹800 + ₹700 = ₹1,500

Device A signs a payment of ₹800 to Bob. Device B signs a payment of ₹700 to Carol. Combined total of ₹1,500 exceeds Alice's ₹1,000 balance!

Reconciliation Gate
Ledger Defense

When connectivity returns, the backend processes Tx A first. When Tx B arrives, it detects insufficient funds, flags `REQUIRES_REVIEW`, and stops overdraft.

Engineering Defense & Limitations Analysis

Why can't offline software prevent double-spending without internet?
In a purely offline environment with independent peer-to-peer devices, no shared global state exists (CAP theorem). When Alice gives Bob a phone screen showing "₹800 signed," it is an encrypted digital promise (promissory note), not instant settlement.

How real-world systems address this:
1. Pre-funded Hardware Wallets (UPI Lite / Secure Element): Real offline UPI stores money inside a tamper-resistant hardware chip (e.g. eSE or UICC). The chip decrements balance on-device and refuses to sign if offline funds are depleted.
2. Mesh-Routed Deferred Settlement: This academic architecture shows how asynchronous mesh hops deliver encrypted payloads to an authoritative gateway while enforcing strict idempotency and ledger consistency.
